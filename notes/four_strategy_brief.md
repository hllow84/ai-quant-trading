## Strategy 1: The 5-Step Option Trading Framework

Day trading and swing trading stock options on a highly focused list of liquid
tickers.

**Step 1: Pick the Right Instrument (Focus).** Do not watch 10+ tickers or chase
random tips. Focus strictly on a handful of liquid, high-volume names that you
know intimately (e.g., NVDA, MU, SPY, QQQ) so you understand their price action
and key levels.

**Step 2: Trade Peak Volume Hours.** Only trade when there is high conviction,
institutional activity, and liquidity. The prime window is the first 90 minutes
of the market open (morning session) and the last 90 minutes before the market
close (PM continuation/power hour). Avoid the "dead hours" of 11:00 AM to 2:00 PM
Eastern, where trading volume stalls and you are likely to get "chopped up".

**Step 3: Run Your Pre-Market Preparation.**

- *Sunday Night Scanning:* Scan charts for relative strength (holding up while
  indices trend down) and relative weakness (trending down while indices trend
  up). Use scanners to filter for Power Earnings Gaps (PEG), high-momentum
  setups, or downside candidates.
- *Analyze the Week:* Identify major weekly earnings reports and macroeconomic
  events (e.g., CPI, PPI, FOMC). Tip: Do not hold large positions during volatile
  events like FOMC; wait for the press conference to finish and trade the clear
  direction that emerges.
- *Plot Levels & Write a Trade Plan:* Mark the prior week's high, Friday's
  high/low, and the 21 EMA. Create a trade plan with specific entry triggers,
  targets, and option contract details (ideally looking for option deltas between
  0.30 and 0.35).

**Step 4: Execute on Specific Entry Triggers.**

- *For Short Positions:* Enter when price rejects a key declining moving average
  (such as the 8-day EMA or 50 SMA). Alternatively, use an intraday qualification
  system (like the TQE system) that fires an entry signal when price breaks the
  15-minute opening range breakdown (ORB) to the downside.
- *For Long Positions (The 30-Minute Pivot):* Look for a stock with relative
  strength pulling back on low volume to a key support level (like the 21 EMA).
  Watch for a bottoming process on the intraday chart. Once the first 30-minute
  green candle forms, place an entry order to buy the moment price breaks above
  the high of that green candle.

**Step 5: Apply Strict Exit Rules.**

- *Stop-Loss:* Decide on your invalidation level before you enter. Set a hard stop
  at a key structural level (like low of day, a close below the 8 EMA on swing
  trades), or limit your option contract drawdown to a maximum of 25%.
- *Take-Profit:* Pre-plan multiple profit-taking levels. Sell half of your
  position at 100% gains to make the remaining portion of the trade completely
  risk-free. Once profits are taken, move your stop-loss to breakeven.
- *Mental Tip:* Hide your live P&L while trading to keep your emotions from
  overriding your pre-planned strategy.

## Strategy 2: The 15-Minute "Sneaky Pivot" Strategy

A simple, rules-based, range-bound strategy that requires only one timeframe
(15-minute) and no indicators.

**Step 1: Plot the Magic Lines.**

- *Range High & Range Low:* Mark the previous day's **absolute highest and lowest
  printed price points**.
- *Swing High & Swing Low:* Minimize your chart, look back to the left, and mark
  the next major high level printed above the Range High, and the next major low
  level printed below the Range Low.

**Step 2: Define Your Buy and Sell Areas.** Agree to only execute trades when the
price reaches one of these four lines.

- The Upper Two Lines (Range High / Swing High): sell-side resistance zone.
- The Lower Two Lines (Range Low / Swing Low): buy-side support zone.

**Step 3: Wait for the Three-Candlestick Framework.**

- *Candle 1 (Opening Range Candle):* The opening 15-minute candle of the day
  should plow **aggressively** directly into one of your key zones (e.g.,
  flushing down into the Swing Low).
- *Candle 2 (The "Sneaky" Candle):* The second 15-minute candle serves to confirm
  a reversal (e.g., if we hit a support line, Candle 2 should print green,
  signaling buyers are stepping in).
- *Candle 3 (The Entry Trigger):* The third candle (typically at the 45-minute
  mark) is your entry. The moment the price crosses and pushes above the high of
  the sneaky candle (for a long) or below the low of the sneaky candle (for a
  short), execute the trade.

**Step 4: Manage the Risk and Targets.**

- *Stop-Loss:* Place your stop-loss immediately below the sneaky candle/low of day
  (for longs) or above the sneaky candle/high of day (for shorts). Trust this
  level as your protection, even if the price chops around for several candles
  before moving.
- *Target:* Target the opposite side of the range (e.g., if you bought near the
  Swing Low, ride the trade back up toward the Range High or Swing High).

## Strategy 3: The S&P 500 Futures Reversal Strategy

Capturing rapid intraday reversals on S&P 500 Futures (ES) or Micro Futures (MES).

**Step 1: Identify Key Zones on the 15-Minute Chart.** Perform top-down analysis.
Look at a 15-minute chart to identify major support and resistance zones where the
market has previously topped or bottomed out multiple times, and drag those zones
to the right.

**Step 2: Monitor the Opening Reversal Timing.** Open your charts at the 9:30 AM
Eastern open. Reversals consistently manifest about 30 minutes after the open
(look specifically at key times like 7:45 AM or 8:00 AM on the west coast). Watch
to see if the market makes a strong, fast flush up or down into your pre-marked
15-minute support or resistance zones. Reversals require high volatility to
succeed.

**Step 3: Look for a Trend Break on the 1-Minute Chart.** Zoom into a 1-minute
chart as price enters your key zone. The current trend must be broken before
entering. You confirm this by drawing a short-term trendline and waiting for it to
break, or by watching the swings of the market shift. Wait for the market to stop
making higher highs/lows (for shorts) and instead break support to establish a
lower high.

**Step 4: Wait for Candlestick Confirmation.** Look for a powerful candlestick
pattern to complete the shift, such as a large engulfing candle or a significant
rejection candle. Crucial rule: wait for the candlestick to fully close (use a
countdown timer on your 1-minute chart) before entering. Enter the trade on the
close of that candle using a limit or market order.

**Step 5: Place Your Stop-Loss and Trail the Trade.**

- *Stop-Loss:* Place your initial stop just past the extreme swing high (for
  shorts) or swing low (for longs) to protect your risk with market structure.
- *Risk Mitigation:* Once the market moves in your favor, move your stop-loss to
  breakeven.
- *Trailing Stop:* Trail your stop behind subsequent market swings (pullbacks) on
  the 1-minute chart to lock in profit as the trade runs.
- *Exit:* Close the position as price approaches a major support or resistance
  zone from the larger 15-minute timeframe. Aim for a minimum of 1:2 or 1:3
  risk-to-reward on every trade.

## Strategy 4: Thematic Catalyst Trend Following (Fund/Position Trading)

Institutional-grade trend following, holding leading growth stocks for weeks to
months.

**Step 1: Check the General Market Trend.** Align your bias with the broader
market. Check the short-term (21 EMA), intermediate (50-day SMA), and long-term
(200-day SMA) moving averages of the major indices. If the indices are in a clear
downtrend or trading below the 200-day SMA, reduce stock exposure to zero or look
to short.

**Step 2: Filter for Leadership and Themes.** Screen for highly liquid,
high-market-cap stocks with a high Average Daily Range (ADR) of 4-6%+ that trend
"linearly" (respecting key moving averages predictably). Focus on hot
secular/thematic narratives (such as AI, memory, optics, space) driven by a
fundamental catalyst (e.g., massive earnings guidance increases, backlogs,
partnerships, or government funding).

**Step 3: Identify Relative Strength During Corrections.** True market leaders
reveal themselves during market corrections. Look for stocks building higher lows,
holding key moving averages, or whose relative strength line is hitting new highs
before price does, even while the major indices are making new lows.

**Step 4: Accumulate the Position.** Look for classic basing structures — flat
bases, cup with handles, or volatility contraction patterns (price fluctuations
getting tighter and tighter, indicating supply has dried up). Accumulate as the
stock carves out the right side of its base or pulls back to key moving averages.
A perfect entry is not required; as long as the theme and group are correct, you
can enter with a logical stop placed below the 8 or 21 EMA.

**Step 5: Apply Risk Management & Sell Into Strength.**

- *Cutting Losses:* If the setup does not work right away, cut the loss quickly
  within 1 to 5 days.
- *Selling Into Climax Moves:* When a stock goes on an explosive run, peel back
  exposure and trim when price stretches 10 to 15 ATRs above its 50-day moving
  average. Accelerate selling on vertical price action, multiple price gaps in a
  row, and massive volume spikes.

  equity universe. Strip both and what remains is the macross/trend family that
